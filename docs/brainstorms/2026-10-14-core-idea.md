# Brainstorm: core idea (2026-10-14, big)

Directors: you and your girlfriend (co-directors). This session was with one director;
the other has not weighed in yet.

## The idea (director's words)
"A relaxing game set in a forest or other natural landscapes, and focus on the
maintenance of the environments to facilitate the happiness of the animals of the area,
with the goal of creating flourishing ecosystems complete with predators, prey and flora."

The player is "an eye in the sky, taking the role of a park ranger in a national park
sent to assess the bio-health of the area."

## The moment in play
The player cleans up human detritus (litter, pollutants) and removes invasive plants.
They watch the animals in the area to see what they need, much like reading the needs
panels in Jurassic World Evolution. A deer needs water, safety and a food source, and
the player makes sure it has access to all three.

## Decided
- **Core test:** "Does this help the player leave the park better than they found it,
  and closer to a balanced ecosystem?" The director's words: players "should feel like
  they made progress towards establishing a balanced ecosystem and that the environment
  is in a better place than it was at the beginning of the session (unless disaster had
  struck in the process)."
- **Tone is relaxing.** This is the director's framing from the first answer.
- **Player role:** a park ranger in a national park, seen from an overhead camera.
- **Core loop:** clean up (litter, pollutants, invasive plants) → observe the animals →
  meet their needs.
- **Animal needs are water, food and safety.**
- **The player meets needs directly**, for example by placing a pond to give animals
  water. The director chose "direct" over shaping the land and letting nature respond.
- **Predation is healthy.** "The wolf needs to eat, it's part of maintaining a balanced
  ecosystem." Hunting is not a failure the player tries to prevent.
- **Each kind of animal finds safety in its own way:** herd animals in numbers,
  marsupials in trees to hide in, ground mammals in undergrowth and burrows.
- **Disasters are setbacks, not total destruction.** For example, a wildfire damages
  part of the park and the player helps it recover.

## Rejected and why
- **Shaping the land and letting nature respond** (indirect), e.g. clearing a stream so
  water returns on its own. Offered as a prompt; the director chose direct placement.
- **Safety meaning "no predators nearby".** Ruled out by the decision that predation is
  healthy.

## Open questions for the director
- Is the player a ranger on foot, or is the ranger a story framing for a free overhead
  camera?
- One park, or several parks in different parts of the world? The marsupial example
  suggests an Australian park.
- How do the two co-directors settle a design disagreement?
- How does the player know they are making progress? Is there a visible measure of
  ecosystem health or balance?
- How do new animals arrive? Do they move in on their own when conditions are right, or
  does the player bring them in?
- Is there a session or level structure, such as a park that counts as "finished"?
- Name, platform, engine, and look (art style and palette). None of these have been
  discussed yet.
- What is the other director's take on everything above?
