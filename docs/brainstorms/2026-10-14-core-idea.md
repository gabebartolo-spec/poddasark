# Brainstorm: core idea (2026-10-14 to 2026-10-15, big)

Directors: you (lead director, final say) and your girlfriend (joins later). This
session was with the lead director.

## The idea (director's words)
"A relaxing game set in a forest or other natural landscapes, and focus on the
maintenance of the environments to facilitate the happiness of the animals of the area,
with the goal of creating flourishing ecosystems complete with predators, prey and flora."

The player is "an eye in the sky, taking the role of a park ranger in a national park
sent to assess the bio-health of the area."

Theme, in the director's words: "We are looking at snapshots of life, standing in for
entire species. Our scope is contained but the implications of our actions are
widespread. Helping the wolf at home helps all wolves abroad."

## The moment in play
The first thing the player meets is a lonely wombat in the Australian park. It is scared,
alone, hungry and without shelter. It knows it has friends in the area but isn't sure
where. The player fixes one issue at a time until the wombat finds its friends and
family.

The general loop: the player cleans up human detritus (litter, pollutants) and removes
invasive plants, then watches the animals to see what they need (much like the needs
panels in Jurassic World Evolution) and meets those needs.

## Decided

### Vision and pillars
- **Core test:** "Does this help the player leave the park better than they found it,
  and closer to a balanced ecosystem?" The director's words: players "should feel like
  they made progress towards establishing a balanced ecosystem and that the environment
  is in a better place than it was at the beginning of the session (unless disaster had
  struck in the process)."
- **Tone is relaxing.**
- **Everything in the ecosystem has a job.** Prey, predators and weather events all
  perform essential services to the ecosystem and come together "in a beautiful
  harmony".
- **Predators are never villains.** "They're just doing what is in their nature, and
  the balance of the ecosystem relies on just that." Predation is healthy, not a failure
  the player tries to prevent.

### Player and camera
- **The player controls a free overhead camera.** "Park ranger" is the story framing,
  not a character walking around on foot.
- **The player acts like a ranger.** When a bush fire breaks out, the player calls the
  fire brigade, who come and fight it.

### Animals and needs
- **Animal needs are water, food and safety.**
- **Each kind of animal finds safety in its own way:** herd animals in numbers,
  marsupials in trees to hide in, ground mammals in undergrowth and burrows.
- **Population matters only a bit.** A lonely animal won't thrive. Each species on the
  map is a small group standing in for the whole species, not a full simulated
  population.
- **The player meets needs directly**, for example by placing a pond to give animals
  water. The director chose this over shaping the land and letting nature respond.
- **How animals arrive depends on the scenario.** They may already be there, move in
  on their own, or be brought in by the player.

### Parks, modes and progress
- **The game has several parks around the world. Australia is the first.**
- **There are two modes.** In campaign, the parks come in a fixed order. In free play,
  the player can start in any unlocked park or in a custom environment.
- **A campaign park is done when it passes the harmony gate.** It passes when every
  species reaches a set contentedness level.

### The Australian campaign (each phase zooms out further)
1. **Zoomed-in tutorial.** One species, one issue at a time: the lonely wombat. It ends
   when the wombat finds its friends and family.
2. **Zoomed-out "apply learnings" phase.** A larger area with more species and
   environmental features. No predators yet; the player is eased in gently.
3. **Predator intro.** The camera zooms out again and the dingo arrives, with a small
   tutorial of its own. Its needs are mostly like a prey animal's, but this phase
   teaches the balance needed to keep predators alive.
4. **Further out.** New predators arrive (eagle and crocodile), plus new prey where
   the balance needs it. The **Weather Events** system is introduced. In Australia
   that means bush fires.

### Disasters and weather
- **Disasters are setbacks, not total destruction.**
- **During a bush fire, the player calls the fire brigade.**
- **Recovery is a mix.** The player kick-starts regeneration, then nature does the
  rest. Fire is a natural part of the ecosystem.

### Production
- **Platform:** PC only for now.
- **Release:** itch.io, as both a download and a browser build.
- **Engine:** Godot 4.7.2.
- **Team:** the lead director works on it alone for now, and their girlfriend joins
  later. The lead director has final say on design.

### Session 3 (2026-10-15, during the M1 build)
- **Camera:** the director confirmed the M0 camera feels fine.
- **Realism and learning:** the game should be as true to real animal behaviour as
  possible, leaving room for an educational experience within the structure of a game.
  The director's words: people "should walk away from each play session feeling like
  they understand those animals and their habitats a little better than they did
  before."
- **Wombat tutorial order:** shelter, then scared, then hungry, then alone.
- **How the wombat finds its friends:** the player fixes the land, and they come out.
- **Contentedness display:** a bar in the needs panel and an icon over the animal.
- **Cleanup is part of the wombat tutorial.**
- **Whether placing costs anything:** left open. Placing is free for now, and each
  placeable has a cost value in its data file.

## Rejected and why
- **Shaping the land and letting nature respond** (indirect), e.g. clearing a stream so
  water returns on its own. Offered as a prompt; the director chose direct placement.
- **Safety meaning "no predators nearby".** Ruled out because predation is healthy.
- **A ranger on foot.** The director chose a free overhead camera.
- **Three.js and Unity.** Three.js only gives a browser build and has no editor. Unity
  is heavier to learn than this game needs.

## Open questions for the director
- What happens after the Australian park passes the harmony gate? Which park comes
  next, and does it start zoomed in again? *(Tabled for a later session.)*
- How exactly does the wombat find its friends? Does the player search, follow clues,
  or fix the land so they come out?
- Custom environments in free play need a map editor or generator. Ship free play with
  only the unlocked parks first?
- Contentedness: how is it shown, and what is the gate level?
- What are the species in the phase 2 "apply learnings" area?
- The game's name. "poddasark" is the repo name; is it the title?
- The look (art style and palette). This is not to be decided by AI.
- Which wombat? Common wombats are mostly solitary. A wombat that finds "friends and
  family" fits the southern hairy-nosed wombat better, which lives in group warrens.
  Given the realism goal, which species is the tutorial wombat?
- Whether placing things costs anything.
